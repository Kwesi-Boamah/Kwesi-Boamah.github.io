This paper examines the relationship between Ghana's Electronic Transfer Levy (E-levy) and the stock market performance of targeted firms, notably fintech companies and banks. Using a difference-in-differences approach, we find a negative effect on target firms' stock prices around the implementation period, and a positive price response to the subsequent reduction in the levy rate. Channel tests indicate that the levy affects stock prices through transaction volume, with stronger effects for firms with below-average trading volume.
